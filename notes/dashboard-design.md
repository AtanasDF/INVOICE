## How that is read

**The top of the dashboard is a scanner.** One big scan button, then three smaller ones
under it: Create an invoice · Copy a document · Upload a document. A line beside "Create an
invoice" offers to write one by hand instead.

**Then the people you deal with.** A search across customers and suppliers, with Add a
customer and Add a supplier beside it, and the ones used most recently listed. Each row goes
straight to a new invoice for that customer, already carrying the next number.

**Then three tabs, one page.** Invoices & customers (the default) · Receipts & bills ·
Invoices sent. Nothing navigates away; the tab is remembered.

**Check a company sits lower and smaller**, as he asked.

**A catch-up line for a new account**: scan a few old invoices and the numbering carries on
from yours. Shown only while the account is empty.

## Named, not guessed

1. **Does the big scan button do anything?** He said both "that actually works" and "no,
   that doesn't work" in the same breath. A button that does nothing is a trap, so it is
   built as the main scan — a receipt or a bill, the commonest thing anyone does. One line
   to change if he meant it as decoration.
2. **"Copy a document is going to be the created document"** is read as: copying is the
   plain scan-to-PDF path, with no AI reading and nothing stored — which is what `/copy`
   already does.
3. **The third tab, "invoices already uploaded"**, is read as the invoices *he* has issued,
   since scanned supplier invoices are bills and belong in the second tab.
